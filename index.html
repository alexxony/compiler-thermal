Compiler-Thermal

A compiler-optimization loop that judges GPU kernel transformations by simulated hotspot ΔT (K) from an RC thermal model, not latency or raw energy — the same measurement-driven rule engine as the sibling latency-axis project, with the objective swapped.

Why iso-work is the right comparison: it asks "how much heat to finish the same amount of work," not "how much heat while running flat-out forever." At 100% duty (saturated, non-realistic — nothing else ever runs), TF32's higher instantaneous power makes it look worse; iso-work credits it for finishing sooner and idling after. Both duty regimes are reported in the README's Limits section — this isn't hidden in a footnote, it's the headline's load-bearing assumption made adjustable by hand.

Limits / not proven

  • No physical measurement — simulation vs. simulation. Every ΔT and energy figure comes from measured GPU power/traffic signals fed through a compact RC thermal model, cross-checked against Ansys Twin Builder simulation output. No thermocouple or die-shot ground truth anywhere in this chain.
  • The iso-work duty cycle (15.66%) is a chosen comparison basis, not the only valid one, and it is load-bearing — the slider above makes that adjustable by hand rather than asserting it in a footnote.
  • KernelBench ablation is 40% at the pre-registered criterion. The 87.5%/7-of-8 figure is a bucket reclassification of the same v3 results, not a re-measurement.
  • Hotspot amplification (1.23×–1.64×) contradicts the pre-registered expectation (attenuation was hypothesized going in) — reported as observed, at both a 16 W and an independent 30 W condition, not adjusted to fit the hypothesis.
  • No claim of identical methodology across all three sibling repos. This repo and hbm-build document pre-registered hypotheses with recorded reversals and sha256-checked reproducibility; gpu-solver-loop does not have documented evidence of either practice.
  • Independent verification here means re-derivation from raw ledger files by a separate reviewing pass within this project, not an external, disinterested third party.
